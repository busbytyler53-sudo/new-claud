The name

Sottovoce. Said quietly, worn loudly.

Amici means friends. The Culture Club sells belonging. Both work because the name is a feeling you buy into, and the logo is the membership card. Sottovoce plays the same game with a sharper hook: it's quiet luxury in one word, at a moment when quiet luxury is the whole market.

It's Italian, so it sits next to Amici and C.P. Company on the rail without feeling like a copy. It's also a real music term (the marking that tells a player to drop to a hush), which gives you years of campaign material: vinyl, late-night bars, the Riviera in October.

The short name

Three letters for the regulars. Two for the cap.

S.V.C. is what people will actually call it, the same way Culture Club becomes TCC. It goes where the full name won't fit: a hoodie spine, a sleeve hit, a sub-line like "S.V.C. Sport". Dots stay in. They make it read as a club, not a logistics company.

The VS monogram is the icon. A Bodoni V with an italic S running through it, with a hairline gap where they cross so it still reads as two letters when embroidered small. This is the one that goes on caps, buttons and your profile picture.

Before you print anything

The boring part that saves the brand.

  1. Run a trademark search for SOTTOVOCE in class 25 (clothing) with the USPTO, UKIPO and EUIPO. A quick web check found no clothing label with this name, but that is not legal clearance. A perfume called Sotto Voce exists in class 3, which shouldn't block clothing.
  2. Buy the domain and the handles the same day you decide. They go fast once a name is posted anywhere.
  3. Send the SVG files to a digitiser and get a stitch-out of the script mark and monogram at real size before ordering stock. Hairlines in Bodoni need thickening for embroidery under about 4cm.
  4. Pay a designer for a day to hand-refine the monogram crossing and the script's S. These files are a strong starting point; a final mark always gets one human pass.
