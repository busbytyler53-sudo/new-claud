The story

Twelve rooms, one month, every year.

Every August, Italy empties out. The cities shut, the shutters come down, and the whole country drives to the sea. Families go back to the same town, the same pensione, the same umbrella in the same row, year after year. The waiter remembers how you take your coffee. The kid from room 12 is now the one teaching the new kids to dive off the rocks.

Agosto is that hotel. Twelve rooms on a cliff above the water, a lemon tree on the terrace, two palms at the end of the beach, swallows nesting under the roof. It's open one month a year and it's full every night of it.

It doesn't exist. We made it up. But anyone who's had a summer like that knows exactly where it is, and that's what we sell: the clothes you'd find there. What the guests wear. What the staff wear. What you bring home.

Ci vediamo ad agosto.

The name

Agosto, and Ago. for short.

In Italy, August is less a month than a place you go. Ferragosto, the 15th, is the biggest holiday of the year, and the whole country is on the coast for it. The name carries the whole story in one word, and it shortens on its own.

Italianago

A needle. The tailor's tool. A good reason for the short name to sit on the chest of a well-made tee.

Before you print anything

The boring part that saves the brand.

  1. Run a class 25 (clothing) trademark search for AGOSTO with the USPTO, UKIPO and EUIPO. A web search found no clothing label using it, only similar names like August and Augusta The Brand, but that isn't legal clearance.
  2. Keep the hotel a story, not a claim. Don't print "since 1958" or describe it as a real place. "A hotel we made up" is more charming, and it keeps you on the right side of advertising rules.
  3. Grab the handles and domain the day you decide: @agosto, @agosto.hotel, @ago.agosto, agosto.club.
  4. Sample everything first. One stitch-out of the swallow and script at real size, one printed sample of each souvenir tee. Bodoni hairlines need thickening for embroidery under about 4cm.
