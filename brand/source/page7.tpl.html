The story

Every day has a rhythm. Dress for all of it.

Ritmo is Italian for rhythm. Every good day has one: the early session, the commute, the work, the dinner, the walk home. The trouble is most wardrobes break the rhythm. Gym kit that only works at the gym. Work clothes that only work at work. One good shirt saved for going out.

Ritmo is one wardrobe that keeps time with the whole day. Same palette, same clean cuts, same quiet branding, so the training short works with the knit polo and the evening shirt works with the hoodie. You don't stop to change who you are between 9am and 9pm. You just keep moving.

That's why one brand can do activewear, smart casual, date night and streetwear without feeling like four labels. The niche is the rhythm.

The names

Ritmo, R. and a metronome that keeps time.

Ritmo is already short, two syllables, easy in any accent. The monogram and the metronome give you ways to brand a piece with no words on it at all.

{{STAMP}}

In Tempo. Italian for "in time" and "on time". The sign-off on hang tags, packaging, back prints and every email.

The lines

Four lines, one look.

Each line is a beat in the day, so the range explains itself on the website. Same colours and the same branding rules across all four, which is what stops it feeling like a brand with no niche.

The base

One tee, six colours, under everything.

The heavyweight tee sits across all four lines: under the overshirt, under the hoodie, on its own at the gym. It's the piece people buy first and buy again. Tonal wordmark, one shade off the cloth.

Marks

Six marks, all quiet.

One wide sans for everything. The metronome is the only drawing in the system. Branding gets quieter as the day goes on: bold on Strada, small on Sport, label only on Giorno and Sera.

Colour

Six colours. Everything goes with everything.

The same six colours across all four lines, so a customer can mix a Sport short with a Strada hoodie and a Giorno polo and it always works. The logo is the garment colour, one shade darker, or one shade lighter on Olive, Navy and Black.

{{SWATCHES}}

Before you order

Do these first.

  1. Get a class 25 trademark search done for RITMO. The only US clothing filing I found (Ritmo Latino) was cancelled in 2013, and Rytme, a Danish running label, is spelled differently. Still, get it confirmed properly before you spend.
  2. Grab the handles and domain the same day: @ritmo, @ritmo.studio, @ritmoclub, ritmo.studio.
  3. Activewear is a different factory from cut-and-sew basics. Find a separate supplier for Sport and test stretch, squat-proofing and sweat-wicking before launch.
  4. Sample everything, wash it three times, and check shrinkage before committing to a run.
